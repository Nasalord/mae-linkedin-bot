# LinkedIn niche research for MAE (Making Auto Easy)

**What this is:** a read-only study of what gets engagement for people and companies like MAE and Vivian Liu, so her drafts stop recycling her own old posts.
**Collected:** 2 Nov 2026, from AJ's logged-in LinkedIn (activity feeds and company post pages). Nothing was liked, commented on, followed or messaged.
**Data file:** `niche-posts.csv` has 62 posts from 15 accounts, each with URL, date, format, reactions, comments, reposts, word count, opening line and a one-line reason.

## How to read the numbers

- **Counts are exact, not estimates.** Reactions, comments and reposts were read from the data LinkedIn sends to the page, as of 2 Nov 2026. Dates come from the post ID (UTC).
- **Coverage is partial.** Only some posts on each feed could be tied to their counts (about 15% to 80% per account), so a few high performers may be missing. Company pages loaded only the last 2 to 6 weeks: Car Dealership Guy, Numa and Automotive News are recent snapshots, not 12-month bests. Jim Fitzpatrick's feed reached back only 4 months.
- **Format** is marked `unverified` where it wasn't checked whether the post had an image or video.
- **Posts described from the page text only** (no URL) have counts marked "~" and are not in the CSV.
- **Accounts:** Greg Ah Sue had one non-automotive post in 12 months, so he was dropped. No Matador founder profile was found. Podium was skipped.

## 1. Topics that win with dealership people

Ranked by what consistently beat each account's own average:

1. **Customer results with real numbers and a named store type.** Ari Polakof's "A CDJR dealership in the Bay Area saw these results in their first month with Flai:" got 156 reactions and 10 comments. His "one of the biggest dealership locations in California" post got 136 reactions. Both have before/after numbers, a GM quote, and no adjectives. These posts beat his integration and partnership posts.
2. **Milestones that belong to people, not products.** Funding (Ari: 820/256 and 503/92), a company moment told as gratitude (Aharon: 470/111), an executive change (Automotive News: 1,428/126, by far its best post that month), a work anniversary (Lowitz: 102/36). The comments are congratulations, which means people comment where there is a person to talk to.
3. **The founder's personal life, done sparingly.** Glenn Lundy's "We got married 15 years ago today." got 130/21. His daily leadership posts got 0 to 18 reactions. His 21-word post about needing glasses got 52 reactions and 13 comments.
4. **One industry stat with a dealer takeaway.** Car Dealership Guy's "Nearly 1 in 3 cars on U.S. roads is 16+ years old:" got 78/15. It stacks the numbers, adds one line on what it means for fixed ops, and names a source.
5. **Naming a category or a belief.** Jay Vijayan's "The AI-Native Dealership…" got 375 reactions and 36 reposts, the most reposts of any vendor post in the set. Devin Daly's "When we created the Conversational AI category…" got 162/8 and 17 reposts. People share an idea they can repeat.
6. **Live operational pain.** "BREAKING: Honda just took its iN dealer system back offline…" (CDG, 40/7). Dealers react to what is breaking their month.
7. **Praising a named peer.** Glenn's post about VinCue's founder (85/4) did better than his own leadership posts, because the person he praised has a network that engages.

**What dealership audiences ignore:** feature lists, integration announcements on their own (about 120 to 150 reactions but under 10 comments), slogans, show plugs and generic motivation.

## 3. Formats and length

- **Plain text from a person beats everything from a company page.** Numa's best post got 92 reactions and 3 comments; its posts drew 0 to 3 comments each. Toma's best was a Thanksgiving team post (54/2). Founders' text posts drew 10 to 256 comments.
- **Two lengths work:**
  - **Short (5 to 60 words) with a photo** for personality and events: "Look mom…" (8 words), "If you're here, let's connect!" (5 words, 62 reactions), the glasses post (21 words).
  - **Medium (70 to 240 words)** for results and stories: Ari's results posts (72 and 95 words), Jaspar's billboard (139), Glenn's anniversary (186).
- **Long posts of 400+ words flop** unless they are a milestone. Aharon's feature-drop lists (441 and 461 words) got 49 to 71 reactions, against 470 for his 234-word milestone.
- **Layout that works:** a one-line hook, a blank line, stacked numbers or 3 to 5 list lines, then a one-line takeaway. CDG adds a "(Source: …)" line, which makes a stat post credible.
- **Video:** none of the top posts matched were video.

## 4. How vendors mention their product without sounding like an ad

1. **The customer's numbers do the selling.** Ari never describes features in his results posts. He gives the store type, first-month numbers, a GM quote, and "No new staff. No overnight process changes."
2. **A belief first, the product as proof.** Devin opens with the category belief; the product news comes second.
3. **The customer's words as the hook.** Numa opens with a GM's question and a customer's "How's my truck coming along?"
4. **Insight from the product's own data.** Jaspar's "10x more likely to respond" is product data presented as a market insight, and it got 103/21. This is the model for MAE's transcripts. The rule: one new insight per post, never the same dataset re-told.
5. **Let a third party say it.** Griffin's best product-adjacent post was a repost of press coverage that reframed his category.
6. **Keep the company name to a minimum.** In Ari's CDJR results post, "Flai" appears in the opening line and once in the numbers; everything else is about the customer.

**What reads as an ad (and underperforms):** "Thrilled to announce [integration]" (about 120 to 150 reactions, under 10 comments), slogans ("AI should protect revenue without sacrificing the customer experience.", 13/0), and plain product descriptions ("Barry is the AI ops manager for…", 20/2).

## 5. CTAs that get comments

- **A milestone or gratitude post.** People comment congratulations (Aharon 111, Ari 256, Automotive News exec change 126). No question is needed.
- **Naming and thanking people.** Lowitz's anniversary post thanked colleagues and got 36 comments on 102 reactions.
- **A light, specific either/or.** Veronica's "Austin question: comfy or dressy?" got ~43 reactions and ~12 comments (page text only).
- **A short opinion people can agree or disagree with.** Jim Fitzpatrick's Chinese-cars post got 8 comments on 7 reactions.
- **Small vulnerability.** Glenn's glasses post got 13 comments on 52 reactions.

**What did not work:** a generic leadership question tacked onto the end ("What's the ONE thing that makes this week a win for you?"). Glenn ends almost every daily post this way and gets 0 to 1 comments. "Link in first comment" did not save weak posts either.

## 6. What flops

- Rebrands or "next chapter" with no story: "Excited for this next chapter." (Lowitz, 15/0)
- Show or podcast plugs: "If you haven't already, check out our new show…" (13/0); interview promos (15 to 22 reactions)
- Press thank-yous: "Thanks to the Auto Remarketing team for highlighting…" (Toma, 7/0)
- Slogan openers and motivational titles: "Confidence Changes the Conversation." (Katie Naughton, 18/0)
- Templated daily series: "Monday Pillar Check-In." and "Sunday Reset." (Glenn, 0 to 4 reactions)
- Feature-drop lists and integration announcements (reach without conversation)
- Generic stats without a dealer angle: "70% of outbound campaigns fail." (Jaspar, 54, about half his specific-number posts)

## 7. Gaps nobody covers that MAE could own

1. **The sales floor itself.** Every dealer-AI voice studied (Flai, Numa, Toma, Impel, Fullpath) posts about phones, leads, service scheduling or marketing. Nobody posts about what happens face to face in the showroom. MAE's recorded sessions are the only data source in this set from that conversation.
2. **The salesperson's point of view.** Posts are aimed at GMs, owners and investors. Nobody writes for or about the 25-year-old salesperson in their first 90 days, even though turnover is a known dealer pain and part of Vivian's stated ICP.
3. **Women as car buyers, with data.** The women-in-automotive accounts cover careers and events. None of them posts on how women are treated in the buying conversation.
4. **Honest AI adoption on the floor.** Vendors post wins. Nobody posts about what floor staff resist, ignore or work around after rollout.
5. **Canada.** None of the studied accounts posts about Canadian dealers. Vivian is Vancouver-based.
6. **An insider who left the OEM to build.** Most AI founders here came from outside auto (Aharon jokes about not knowing what an "Up" was). Vivian's GM background is an authority none of them have.

**Crowded, so avoid competing head-on:** phone answering and service-appointment AI, funding-style announcements, and "AI-native dealership" category language, which Tekion now owns.

## Playbook for Vivian

### Content pillars (6)

1. **Floor Notes:** one new pattern from real showroom conversations per post (anonymised), for example what customers ask, when deals stall, or what top reps say differently. This is the transcript-insight format, with one fresh finding each time and never a repeat of the service-revenue story.
2. **Dealer Results:** Ari's structure. Store type and region, first 30/60/90-day numbers, one GM or salesperson quote, "no new staff" style constraints. Only with real numbers the dealer has approved.
3. **People on the Floor:** spotlight a named salesperson, BDC rep or GM (with permission). The employee-story format drew 158/38 for Jaspar, and named people pull their own networks in.
4. **Insider vs. Outsider:** Vivian's GM years against startup life, and what the OEM side never sees about the store. This is authority the competitors lack.
5. **One Stat, One Takeaway:** CDG's format. One external industry number, stacked figures, one line on "what this means on the sales floor", and a source.
6. **Founder Life (sparingly):** one personal milestone or vulnerable moment a month at most. The data says this lands, but only when it is rare and real.

### Cadence

- **Tuesday:** Floor Notes or One Stat (insight post, 80 to 180 words, text only).
- **Thursday:** Dealer Results or People on the Floor (photo of the real people, 70 to 150 words).
- **Every second Saturday or around events:** a short personal or event post (under 60 words, photo).
- Three posts a week at most. Accounts posting daily (CDG, Glenn) show reach spread thin. Spend 15 minutes on posting days commenting on Ring 1b accounts (CDG, Women In Automotive, Glenn Lundy, Automotive News people posts), where her name reaches GMs.
- Post from Vivian's personal profile and repost to the MAE page, not the reverse: company-page posts drew 0 to 3 comments here.
- The weekly bot starts with one post a week (the Tuesday insight slot or the Thursday people slot, alternating). Add more slots once one a week is running smoothly.

### 10 hook templates

1. "[Store type] in [region] [did X] in their first [30] days:" (then numbers)
2. "[Number] [things/customers/reps]. [One surprising result]." (example shape: "[N] showroom conversations. [N] asked the same question first.")
3. "[Date]. [What the industry looked like then]." (the time-stamp opener for anniversaries)
4. "Our [role, e.g. first dealer / top rep] just [milestone]."
5. "[Person] told me: “[exact quote]”" (customer or GM quote as line 1)
6. "Nearly [fraction] of [dealer thing] [surprising fact]:" (One Stat format, with source)
7. "Every [GM/salesperson] knows [obvious thing]. Almost nobody [measures/does] [other thing]."
8. "[X] isn't a [common label]. It's a [reframe]." (category belief, for example "The guest sheet isn't paperwork. It's [reframe].")
9. "I spent [N] years at [GM]. Here's what I never saw from the OEM side:"
10. "[Short, slightly absurd true thing]. :)" (8 to 15 words plus a photo; use rarely)

### Do's and don'ts

**Do**
- Put a number, a name or a quote in line 1.
- Let the customer's numbers carry product posts, and mention MAE once.
- Tag and thank real people by name: it is the most reliable comment driver in this data.
- End with a specific either/or or a short opinion, or with no question at all.
- Cite sources on stat posts.
- Keep insight posts under about 200 words.

**Don't**
- Don't post feature lists, integration announcements or slogans as standalone posts.
- Don't add a generic "What do you think?" or leadership question.
- Don't reuse a dataset or story already posted. The July 2026 "$700,000 in service revenue" post (1,300 transcripts, 27%) is retired, as are the ICP/firing-a-customer, rebuilt-product, ASOTU, investor-room and Techstars stories.
- Don't post video-only promos or podcast plugs.
- Don't compete on phone or service-AI language (Flai, Numa, Toma and Impel own it).
- Don't use "AI-native dealership" (Tekion's phrase).

### 15 fresh post ideas (none reuse her existing posts)

Every number below is a placeholder. Fill it only with real MAE data.

1. **Floor Notes: the first question.** "We looked at [N] showroom conversations. The first thing customers asked wasn't price." Share the actual most common opening question and what top reps say next.
2. **Floor Notes: the 10-minute mark.** At what point in a showroom conversation do deals stall, and what the reps who close say at that moment (pattern only, no names).
3. **Dealer Results (with permission).** "A [brand] store in [city] replaced the paper guest sheet. First 30 days: [captured ups before → after], [CRM entries], [manager follow-ups]." Add the GM quote.
4. **People on the Floor.** Spotlight one salesperson in their first 90 days using Mae: what changed for them, in their words, with photo and tag.
5. **Insider vs. Outsider.** "I spent [N] years at General Motors. Here's what the OEM side never sees about a Saturday on the sales floor."
6. **One Stat, One Takeaway.** Pick a public turnover or tenure stat for dealership salespeople (cite the source), then one line on what it means for training and memory on the floor.
7. **Women as buyers, with data.** From anonymised sessions: what's different in conversations where a woman is the primary buyer (questions asked, time to first price mention). Only if the data supports it.
8. **What reps ignore.** An honest post on which part of Mae floor staff stopped using after week two, and what the team changed. This covers the adoption gap no vendor talks about.
9. **The guest sheet obituary.** A short, slightly absurd image post: a photo of a stack of paper guest sheets and an 8 to 15 word caption.
10. **The GM quote opener.** Start with the most surprising thing a GM said to Vivian this quarter, verbatim, then two lines of context and an either/or question for other GMs.
11. **Canadian dealer angle.** One thing Canadian stores do differently from US stores on the floor, from her own visits, ending with a specific question to Canadian dealers.
12. **Time-stamp anniversary.** The date of MAE's first live showroom session: "[Date]. [What the floor looked like then]." Thank the first store and reps by name.
13. **One Stat: Automotive News or CDG data.** React to one fresh public number, such as average age of vehicles, traffic or used prices, with the sales-floor implication nobody else draws.
14. **Category belief.** One sentence MAE wants to own, stated plainly: for example "AI on the sales floor should make the salesperson better, not replace the conversation." Then three short proofs from real customers.
15. **Event post with personality.** For the next conference: under 40 words, one honest detail, a photo, and "If you're here, let's connect" plus a specific place and time.
